\section*{Conclusions that remain valid}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \cl{Coupling decides the ranking.} At a common temperature the integrations differ only in
routing, and Z2 leads Z1 (\WrIsoGapHiZtwo~W at 100\degC); under a shared cooling the converter's
thermal path reorders them (Sections~\ref{sec:compare-elec}--\ref{sec:compare-b}; ablation in
Section~\ref{sec:compare-ablation}).
\item \cl{A top plate alone does not serve the board-backside regulator,} now a feasibility verdict:
GaN junction \TjAZthree\degC{} against \MthLimGan\degC, and its capacitor, inductor, dielectric,
encapsulant and static-drop limits fail as well (Section~\ref{sec:compare-a}).
\item \cl{With a second plate Z2 leads in loss and temperature, Z1 in warpage and displacement.} Z2 is
\GapBZtwo~W and \dTmaxTierBZtwo~K lower inside the package and \WrPlatGapB~W lower with feed and
pumping; Z1 warps \WarpBZone{} against \WarpBZtwo~\textmu m and its critical joint moves \DuBZone{}
against \DuBZtwo~\textmu m. All are resolved, and neither plate-interface bound changes them
(Section~\ref{sec:compare-b}). Z2 now also has the smaller joint response (new conclusion~2).
\item \cl{The embedded regulator needs input decoupling outside its stack.} Bare decoupling costs
\RiseSoneZone~W more than ideal decoupling and puts \WrBgaBareAtNmaxZone~A on each 48-V ball even at
\WrBgaNmax{} balls per rail; interleaving and the ring reach $k_{\mathrm{rms}} = \FrKrms$
(Section~\ref{sec:evaluate-decap}).
\item \cl{Peak temperature is not a proxy for the joint.} Under one top plate Z2 peaks
\WrdTmaxAZtwo~K hotter than Z1, while its ball of largest displacement runs \WrdTjointAZtwo~K cooler
and moves \WrDuRatioAZtwo{} times as far (Section~\ref{sec:compare-a}). The joint response itself now ranks the
other way (new conclusion~2).
\item \cl{Omitted converter losses change no ranking of feasible candidates.} They move the Z1--Z2
gap by at most \WrOmitGapAHi~W under cooling~A (gap \GapAZtwo~W) and \WrOmitGapBHi~W under
cooling~B (gap \GapBZtwo~W) (Section~\ref{sec:explore-omit}); their effect on Z1's margins and on
the count is conditional (below).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Conclusions that become conditional}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \cl{Z1 against Z2 under one top plate depends on the boundary and on the decoupling.} Inside
the package Z2 is lower by \GapAZtwo~W (resolved), but only while its feed stays below
$R^* = \RstarTierAZtwo$~m$\Omega$; with the ball-ring feed (\FeedRbgaZtwo~m$\Omega$) and the pumping
charged, Z1 is lower (\PlatAZone{} against \PlatAZtwo~W). With ideal decoupling Z1 is lower inside
the package too, by \LossGapZtwoSzero~W (Sections~\ref{sec:compare-a} and~\ref{sec:explore-robust}).
\item \cl{Z2's feasibility under one top plate depends on the plate interface.} Against a rigid
plate on the free warpage it converges at \FbAZtwoTmax\degC, beyond the GaN rating, with its die at
\WrFbAZtwoDie\degC; only Z1 stays feasible, its on-resistance margin cut to
\WrFbAZoneRonMargin~K (Section~\ref{sec:compare-a}).
\item \cl{The feasibility of Z1 and Z2 rests on \BgaTarget~A per ball}~\pcite{Kuan2015Imax}: at
\BgaTargetSens~A no legal arrangement closes (Section~\ref{sec:explore-bga}).
\item \cl{Z1's margins} (\WrRonMarginAZone{} to \WrDieMarginAZone~K under cooling~A) are consumed by
a core-heavy die map (die at \WrHotAZoneDie\degC), by the upper omitted-loss estimate (on-resistance fit,
capacitor class, solder-law range) and almost by a rigid plate (Sections~\ref{sec:explore-omit}
and~\ref{sec:explore-die}).
\item \cl{The count optimum} $M = \FrDseOptM$ holds for the lower and nominal omitted-loss
estimates; the upper estimate prefers $M = \WrOmitDseOptHi$, which has no square grid
(Section~\ref{sec:explore-omit}). On a frequency grid \FrFswFineFactor{} times finer no count changes
its topology or parts, but the runner-up, $M = \FrFswFineRunnerUp$ (no square grid), comes within
\FrFswFineRunnerUpMargin~W (Section~\ref{sec:explore-count}).
\item \cl{Every joint ranking} holds under the \MvLawN-law band, but each law is extrapolated in
stress and none is benchmarked against a measured joint (Section~\ref{sec:local}).
\item \cl{Robustness.} \WrRobReversed{} of \WrRobN{} verdicts reverse under a declared alternative
(a second plate under Z2 moves loss and peak toward Z2, the rigid plate moves the loss toward Z1),
and \WrRobPending{} await a mesh ladder of their own. The joint column judges the joint response, at
the \MvScrK{} worst sites of each reference case, and in full for the \MvScrEscN{} variants whose
maximum fell deep among them (those sites alone fell short by up to \MvScrEscShortMax\,\%); the
Z1--Z2 joint ranking holds for every decision with a verdict, although a tenth of the converter-tier conductivity, the low-CTE mold compound and the
core-heavy die map reverse the ranking of the largest ball displacement
(Section~\ref{sec:explore-robust}).
\item \cl{Z1's warpage lead depends on the mounting:} a clamp reacted by a backplate and, under
cooling~B, pinned screws or a three-times-larger board with a cold rim take it away. Board size,
mounting and clamp change the joint responses by \MvScrBdEpsLo{} to \MvScrBdEpsHi\,\%. On the
declared coupon, and under every encapsulant and ball-layer variant, Z1's joints stay more responsive
than Z2's; a three-times-larger board with a 25-$^\circ$C rim reverses that under cooling~A, so the
rim temperature of a real board conditions the cooling-A joint ranking
(Section~\ref{sec:explore-robust}).
\item \cl{Tier-order realizability} is geometric feasibility under declared chip-embedding rules,
and it now requires copper posts: every order crosses the capacitor tier at an aspect ratio of
\MthWorstAR, and still \MthThinAR{} with a 100-\textmu m capacitor (Section~\ref{sec:realizability}).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Conclusions withdrawn}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \cl{``The embedded regulator has the lowest loss under one top plate.''} Inside the package Z2
is now lower by \GapAZtwo~W; Z1 leads only at the platform boundary (conditional~1).
\item \cl{``Under two plates Z3 leads Z1 by 18.1~W.''} With the redistribution coupled the gap
(\PlossBZthree{} against \PlossBZone~W) is not resolved, and Z3 fails its static drop
(Section~\ref{sec:compare-b}).
\item \cl{``76 cells are the optimum.''} Under the corrected synthesis 76 cells admit no four-tier
member (Section~\ref{sec:dse-count}).
\item \cl{``The repartitioned tier order lowers the worst via aspect ratio from 5.3 to 0.83.''} It
relied on a 100-\textmu m capacitor tier that no rated part provides (Table~\ref{S:tab:supp-capev}).
\item \cl{``About 0.5~\textmu F per cell decouples the input.''} $k_{\mathrm{rms}}\le1.2$ needs about
\WrCkTwelve~\textmu F per cell, which the stack cannot hold (Section~\ref{sec:evaluate-decap}).
\item \cl{``The joint of largest displacement is the critical joint.''} In Z2 the critical joint
responds \MvCritRatioAZtwo{} times as much (Table~\ref{S:tab:supp-critv2}).
\item \cl{``Z1 has the smallest joint response''} and \cl{``Z3's joint responds more than Z1's under
two plates.''} Z1's response is the largest under both coolings and every law; under cooling~B Z1
\MvEpsBZone{} against Z3 \MvEpsBZthree$\times10^{-3}$ per cycle (Table~\ref{tab:compare}).
\item \cl{``The omitted converter losses act alike in every architecture.''} They differ through
temperature (Section~\ref{sec:explore-omit}).
\item \cl{``S2 has no operating point.''} It converges and fails the range of the solder laws
(Table~\ref{S:tab:supp-scen}).
\item \textbf{``Fully coupled''}, the categorical novelty claims, ``every reduced model is verified'' and
the 1.1-\% mesh-change criterion.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{New conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \cl{The count is an output.} The framework selects \FrDseOptM{} cells (\FrConvTopo{} at
\FrConvFsw~kHz in \FrConvTierMax{} mixed tiers); its neighbouring square grids lose
\FrChainMarginLo--\FrChainMarginHi~W through the chain, fail the feasibility stage under
cooling~A, and their hotter critical joints respond more under every law of the band
(Section~\ref{sec:explore-count}).
\item \cl{Z1 has the smallest warpage but the largest joint response.} On the declared board it
warps \WarpAZone{} and
\WarpBZone~\textmu m, yet its joint responds \MvEpsAZone{} and \MvEpsBZone$\times10^{-3}$ per cycle
under the production law, against at most \MvEpsAZtwo{} and \MvEpsBZthree{} for the others, and it
stays the largest under every law of the band. Its critical joint
is the hottest (\MvTsjAZone\degC{} against at most \MvTsjAZthree\degC{} elsewhere under cooling~A)
and carries the largest deforming displacement (\MvDudefAZone{} against at most
\MvDudefAZthree~\textmu m), whereas the bending of Z2 and Z3 partly turns into rigid rotation of the
joint. Neither the warpage nor the largest ball displacement orders the responses: a low-CTE mold
compound changes Z1's largest ball displacement by \MvEncEmcDuAZone\,\% but its response by
\MvEncEmcEpsAZone\,\% (Sections~\ref{sec:compare-joints} and~\ref{sec:explore-cards}).
\item \cl{Thermocompression-bonding coplanarity fails for Z1 and Z2:} bonding gaps of \MvRfTcbZone{}
and \MvRfTcbZtwo~\textmu m against \MvTcbLim~\textmu m; a low-CTE mold compound brings Z1 only to
\MvRfTcbEmcZone~\textmu m. At the reflow peak Z2 warps \MvRfCoplZtwo{} times the coplanarity limit
(Section~\ref{sec:compare-assembly}).
\item \cl{Z3 is infeasible under both coolings:} under cooling~A by its ratings and its drop
(\VdropAZthree~mV), under cooling~B by its static drop alone (\VdropBZthree{} against
\MthLimDrop~mV), with its 1-V balls on \BgaSiteFracZthree\,\% of the sites
(Section~\ref{sec:compare-b}).
\item \cl{Under cooling~A Z2 is lower inside the package, Z1 with the feed and pumping} (\GapAZtwo~W;
\PlatAZone{} against \PlatAZtwo~W): the loss boundary decides (Section~\ref{sec:compare-a}).
\item \cl{The integration bounds the converter.} The five-tier converter saves
\FrTfiveSaveLo--\FrTfiveSaveHi~W but protrudes \FrProtrudeTfive~\textmu m below the Z2 board
(Section~\ref{sec:explore-codesign}).
\item \cl{The die limit binds under a core-heavy die map:} only Z2 under cooling~B keeps its die within
\MthLimDie\degC{} (\WrHotBZtwoDie\degC; Section~\ref{sec:explore-die}).
\item \cl{The fine-pitch layers carry the largest shear drivers:} Z1's die-to-converter microbumps
carry \WrGamRatioAZone{} times the driver of its ball of largest $\Delta u$, and Z2's pillars exceed its balls
under cooling~A; they are screened, not ranked, because the microbump driver does not converge at the
die-edge corner (Sections~\ref{sec:evaluate-mech} and~\ref{sec:compare-joints}).
\item \cl{Interleaving is a requirement on the control:} with the ring outside the grid,
\FrDecapPhases{} interleaved clock phases lower $k_{\mathrm{rms}}$ of the package input current from
\WrKsync{} (cells in phase) to \WrKrms; the ring alone gives \WrKring{}
(Section~\ref{sec:evaluate-decap}).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Remaining work for a manufacturable package}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \cl{Capacitor tier.} A qualified copper-post or through-mold-via process for the
\MthTierOne-\textmu m crossing (aspect ratio \MthWorstAR), or a thin flying capacitor rated for
\FrConvCapVone~V at \FrConvCapDerate{} derating that keeps its effective capacitance at bias
(Table~\ref{S:tab:supp-capev}).
\item \cl{Bonding and reflow coplanarity.} A stack, mold compound or attach that brings the bonding
gaps of Z1 and Z2 within \MvTcbLim~\textmu m (now \MvRfTcbZone{} and \MvRfTcbZtwo~\textmu m), and Z2
within the reflow coplanarity.
\item \cl{Ball rating.} Qualification of \BgaTarget~A per 0.4-mm ball at the operating temperature,
or a ball field that closes at \BgaTargetSens~A; Z1's harmonic port-model bound
(\BgaRmsBoundZone~A RMS) resolved with the board network.
\item \cl{Input decoupling.} The \FrDecapPhases-phase interleaved control and the \FrDecapN-capacitor
ring built and measured, including droop and the package-loop inductance
(\WrLloopLo--\WrLloopHi~pH).
\item \cl{Package-backside feed.} A 48-V feed below $R^* = \RstarTierAZtwo$~m$\Omega$ under one top
plate (the ball ring measures \FeedRbgaZtwo~m$\Omega$) or a second plate, and a converter that
stays inside the board opening (the four-tier stack clears it by \FrFitThirtysix~\textmu m).
\item \cl{Board-backside 1-V path.} A static drop within \MthLimDrop~mV per rail: the land side
(\VdropLandBZthree~mV) and the board planes (\VdropPlanesBZthree~mV) add to the drop of the vertical
path, so both sheets need far more copper, and the 1-V balls need sites that leave room for signals.
\item \cl{Plate interface and cooling.} A conforming interface or a die-flatness specification,
cooling for a core-heavy die map, and CFD of the manifold's flow split and headers.
\item \cl{Converter.} A fabricated 1-mm$^2$ inductor at \FrConvIndL~nH and \FrConvIndDcr~m$\Omega$;
circuit-level validation of the synthesized \FrConvTopo{} point and of its omitted losses.
\item \cl{Joints.} A SAC305 law calibrated against measured hysteresis loops, local models of the
microbumps and pillars, and a transient power cycle instead of the prescribed one.
\item \cl{Numerics.} A through-thickness rung on the production ladders, and the
\MthSolverWarp-\% difference between the iterative and direct structural solvers in warpage.
\item \cl{Prototype.} Loss, temperature and warpage measured on a fabricated package.
\end{enumerate}
