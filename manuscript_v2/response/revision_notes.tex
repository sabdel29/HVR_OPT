Every number is generated from the result artifacts of the repository (the same macros as the

\section*{Conclusions that remain valid}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Coupling decides the ranking.} The integrations differ only in routing at a common
temperature (Z2 \GapIsoHotZtwo~W ahead of Z1 at 100\,$^\circ$C); the cooling they share reorders them.
\item \textbf{Z3 is infeasible under the top plate alone.} GaN junction bound \TjAZthree\,$^\circ$C
against the 150-$^\circ$C rating; six component and material limits fail.
\item \textbf{Under two plates Z2 has the lowest loss and Z1 the smallest die warpage and joint
driver}, both resolved under the conservative rule (\PlossBZtwo{} vs \PlossBZone~W; \WarpBZone{} vs
\WarpBZtwo~\textmu m). Z2's lead survives a physical feed (0.6--2~m$\Omega$ against
$R^* = \RstarTierBZtwo$~m$\Omega$), the implemented decoupling and the interface feedback.
\item \textbf{The embedded regulator needs local input decoupling}; without it the joint leaves the
solder law's range (S1) and S2 is infeasible under the intact-solid model. It is now implemented
physically (7 interleaved phases and a ring of 56 1206 capacitors; $k = \DecapKeq$;
\DecapPlossZone~W; joint \DecapTjointZone\,$^\circ$C).
\item \textbf{The repartitioned tier order has the better heat path} in both capacitor cases (A0:
\SensDTierRefZonePloss~W and \SensDTierRefZoneTmax~K; A1: \RcfmaxRefAPloss{} vs \RcfmaxAPloss~W).
\item \textbf{The joint of largest displacement is the critical joint} (\SolderSites{} competing sites
in \SolderCases{} cases; monotone response).
\item \textbf{Temperature is not a proxy for joint response} (Z2: hotter peak, cooler joint, larger
driver).
\item \textbf{Omitted converter losses change no ranking} (\OmitLo--\OmitHi~W per integration;
gaps move by at most 0.2~W).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Conclusions that become conditional}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Tier-order realizability} (aspect ratio 0.83 vs 5.3) holds only with a thin, rated
flying-capacitor technology (A0). With rated MLCCs (A1) neither order meets the 1:1 build-up rule
(8.6 vs 5.3) and the reference order has the smaller crossing.
\item \textbf{Physical feasibility of the embedded stack} is established only when the capacitor
technology, or a redesigned stack, is demonstrated; A1 needs copper posts, and in Z2 the A1
converter no longer fits the 2.4-mm board opening.
\item \textbf{Z1 vs Z2 under one top plate}: Z1 leads by \GapAZtwo~W with ideal decoupling
(resolved with correlation credit), the two tie with implemented decoupling (\DecapPlossZone{} vs
\SdecAZtwoPloss~W), and only Z1 stays feasible against a non-conforming plate.
\item \textbf{Z1 vs Z3 joint response under two plates} is resolved only with Z3's land-side
redistribution coupled (\EpsBZone{} vs \EpsBZthree$\times10^{-3}$); on the uncoupled lower bound it was
not, and the alternative solder law narrowed it to \SchubertGapB\,\%.
\item \textbf{Platform preference for the bottom plate}: Z1 top-cooled (\PlatAZone~W) and Z2
dual-cooled (\PlatBZtwo~W) are within the pump-efficiency band.
\item \textbf{Regulator-count optimum}: the electrical screen keeps its minimum at
$M = \DseCorrOptM$ under the corrected capacitor rule, but that count is not buildable on the square
grid; of the buildable counts, the coupled chain confirms 49 cells over 64 (\PlossZone{} vs
\CountCellPloss--\CountSynPloss~W). The screen optimum is an electrical optimum, not an ETM optimum.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Conclusions withdrawn}
\begin{enumerate}
\item ``Under one top plate the embedded regulator is best in every respect'': electrically it ties
with Z2 once its decoupling is implemented.
\item ``Under two plates Z3 leads Z1 electrically'': with the land-side and board-plane redistribution
coupled, Z3 is behind at 10--40~\textmu m of land-side copper (\ZthreeLandBFortyPloss~W at 40~\textmu m
vs \PlossBZone~W) and ties only at \ZthreeLandTie~\textmu m.
\item ``The synthesized design is implemented by the selected library capacitor'': the 25-V part is
under-rated for the 32-V $C_1$, and no part fits the 100-\textmu m tier.
\item ``About 0.5~\textmu F per cell gives a smooth package input'': $k \le 1.2$ needs about
\DecapCkTwo~\textmu F per cell, which the stack cannot hold.
\item Categorical novelty claims and ``every reduced model is verified''.
\item The 1.1\,\% mesh-change criterion for comparisons, superseded by the per-output budget at the
MOOSE-observed convergence order.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Corrections found during the revision}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The synthesis capacitor rule compared every flying capacitor with $V_{\mathrm{in}}/N$; it now
checks $(N-i)V_{\mathrm{in}}/N$ against the derated rating and the effective capacitance against the
ripple limit. The published rule is kept as a legacy configuration.
\item The cold-plate coefficient counted half the coolant rise twice (\CoolCalK~K per kW); the
correction lowers every loss by 1--5~W and changes no ranking.
\item The cells' internal 48-V runs carry the bare input pulse whatever decoupling sits outside the
cell; the implemented-decoupling runs include it (about \DecapHotLoop~W per integration).
\item The production mesh leaves the converter stack and board 2--3~K cold (MOOSE refinement); the
uncertainty budget was revised accordingly.
\item The local solder card interpolates the creep prefactor linearly between rows 25~K apart
(up to \CardGridMax\,\% in the response); this is now part of $U$.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Remaining work for a fully manufacturable package}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A flying-capacitor technology rated $\ge$40~V with $\ge$0.19~\textmu F ($C_1$) and
$\ge$0.38~\textmu F ($C_2$) effective at bias, temperature and tolerance, in $\le$2.8~mm$^2$ each and
$\le$100--120~\textmu m height (custom silicon or thin-film integrated capacitors), or a converter
redesign at a higher switching frequency that lowers the required capacitance.
\item A vertical interconnect for a thick capacitor tier (copper posts or through-mold vias at a
demonstrated aspect ratio) and its process qualification.
\item For Z2, a board opening at least 2.7~mm deep (or a protruding converter) and a demonstrated
48-V feed below $R^*$.
\item For Z3, land-side copper of at least \ZthreeLandThirtyMv~\textmu m per rail (30-mV drop) and
\ZthreeLandTie~\textmu m to match Z1, a ball pitch of \PitchMaxZthree~mm or a reallocation of
signal balls.
\item The seven-phase interleaved control and the ring capacitance, implemented and measured.
\item A conforming plate interface or lid, with a die-flatness specification.
\item Assembly feasibility (placement, encapsulation, lamination warpage), a solder law calibrated
against measured hysteresis loops, and experimental validation of temperature, loss and warpage on a
prototype.
\item High-frequency power-integrity analysis (droop, resonance) beyond the input network.
\end{enumerate}
