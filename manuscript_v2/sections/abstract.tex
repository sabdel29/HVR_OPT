% ============================================================================ ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Kilowatt processors draw about a kiloampere at 1~V, so distributed vertical power delivery
places many regulator cells beneath the load. Where should these cells be integrated once
loss, temperature and deformation are solved together? Three integrations are
compared: embedded between substrate and die (Z1), on the substrate backside (Z2) and on the
board backside (Z3). An electrothermal--mechanical framework synthesizes the regulator from a
discrete part library and returns the cell count. On one package geometry it
couples a rail-aware loss ledger to temperature, searches every solder joint and checks every
part rating. For a 1-kW, 48-to-1-V load it selects \FrDseOptM{} hybrid switched-capacitor cells
with three capacitor and three inductor branches, switching at \FrConvFsw~kHz. Under one
top cold plate, Z1 and Z2 dissipate within \GapAZtwo~W of each other, while Z3 exceeds its
device ratings. With an added bottom plate, Z2 dissipates \GapBZtwo~W less than Z1 and peaks
\dTmaxTierBZtwo~K cooler, but its die warps \FrWarpRatioB{} times as much. Z3 then lacks ball
sites and exceeds the static voltage-drop limit. The die stays below \DieTmax\,$^\circ$C under
a uniform load but reaches \FrDieHotMax\,$^\circ$C when three quarters of its power
concentrates in its central half. Three requirements follow: interleaved input decoupling,
copper posts through the 1-mm capacitor tier, and a converter short enough for the
package-backside board, at up to \FrTfiveSaveHi~W of extra loss.
\end{abstract}
