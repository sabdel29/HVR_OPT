% ============================================================================ ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Kilowatt processors draw about a kiloampere at 1~V, so distributed vertical power delivery
places many regulator cells directly beneath the load. Where the cells are integrated---between
substrate and die, on the substrate backside or on the board backside---decides which rail
crosses each vertical interconnect, where the converter's heat flows and which solder joints
deform, yet the choice is made one domain at a time on a fixed geometry. This paper presents an
electrothermal--mechanical framework that carries a regulator synthesized from a generalized
hybrid switched-capacitor--buck family into an explicit package and evaluates, on one geometry
and one converged field, a rail-aware loss ledger, an electrothermal fixed point with a
microchannel cold plate, a thermoelastic solution with an all-site joint search, a viscoplastic
solder submodel and interconnect realizability. Each reduced model is verified against a matched
reference; one coupled evaluation takes a few hours. Three modes of use are demonstrated on
a 1-kW, 48-to-1-V system. Evaluation shows that the embedded regulator is admissible only with
local input decoupling and a repartitioned tier order. Comparison shows that coupling reverses
the electrical ranking in both directions: the substrate-backside regulator, ahead by
\DeltaPrtZtwoSzero~W at room temperature, dissipates \LossGapZtwoSzero~W more when coupled,
whereas the board-backside regulator, behind by \DeltaPrtAbsZthreeSzero~W, leads by
\LossGapZthreeSzero~W because its converter runs \dTlsAbsZthreeSzero~K cooler, and warps the die
\WarpFoldZthree{} times less, but its 1-V current needs nearly the entire ball array.
Exploration moves the regulator-count optimum to 76 cells with a different topology and
identifies the package decisions that reverse each ranking.
\end{abstract}
