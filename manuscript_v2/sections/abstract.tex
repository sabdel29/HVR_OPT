\begin{abstract}
Kilowatt processors draw about a kiloampere at 1~V, and distributed vertical power delivery
answers by placing many regulator cells directly beneath the load. Where those cells are
integrated---between the substrate and the die, on the substrate backside, or on the board
backside---decides which rail crosses every vertical interconnect, where the converter's heat
must flow and which solder joints carry the deformation, yet the choice is still made one domain
at a time on a geometry fixed in advance. This paper presents an electrothermal--mechanical
framework that carries a regulator synthesized from a generalized hybrid switched-capacitor--buck
family into an explicit package stack and evaluates, on one geometry and one converged field, a
rail-aware conductor-level loss ledger, an electrothermal fixed point with a microchannel cold
plate, a thermoelastic package solution with an all-site critical-joint search, a viscoplastic
solder submodel and the realizability of the vertical interconnect. Every reduced model is
verified against a matched reference, and one coupled evaluation takes about an hour on a
workstation. Three modes of use are demonstrated on a 1-kW, 48-to-1-V, 49-cell system.
Evaluation shows that the embedded regulator is admissible only with local input decoupling and
a repartitioned tier order. Comparison shows that coupling reverses the electrical ranking: the
backside regulator, ahead by \DeltaPrtZtwoSzero~W at room temperature, dissipates
\LossGapZtwoSzero~W more on the converged field because its converter runs \dTlsZtwoSzero~K
hotter, while the board-backside regulator consumes almost the entire ball-grid array for its
1-V current. % TODO(data): mechanical sentence
Exploration moves the regulator-count optimum to 76 cells with a different topology and
identifies the package decisions that reverse each ranking.
\end{abstract}
