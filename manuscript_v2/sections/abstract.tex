% ============================================================================ ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Kilowatt processors draw about a kiloampere at 1~V, so distributed vertical power delivery
places many regulator cells directly beneath the load. Where the cells are integrated---between
substrate and die, on the substrate backside or on the board backside---decides which rail
crosses each vertical interconnect, where the converter's heat flows and which solder joints
deform, yet the choice is made one domain at a time on a fixed geometry. This paper presents an
electrothermal--mechanical framework that carries a regulator synthesized from a generalized
hybrid switched-capacitor--buck family into an explicit package and evaluates, on one geometry
and one converged field, a rail-aware loss ledger, an electrothermal fixed point with
microchannel cold plates, a thermoelastic solution with an all-site joint search, a viscoplastic
solder submodel and interconnect realizability. Each reduced model is verified against a matched
reference; one coupled evaluation takes a few hours. Three modes of use are demonstrated on a
1-kW, 48-to-1-V system. Evaluation shows that the embedded regulator is admissible only with
local input decoupling and a repartitioned tier order, which alone saves \SensDTierRefZonePloss~W
and \SensDTierRefZoneTmax~K. Comparison at a common temperature, under the same top cold plate
and under the same top and bottom plates shows that coupling decides the ranking: the
board-backside regulator, \GapIsoHotZthree~W behind on routing alone, does not survive top-only
cooling, and with bottom cooling the backside regulators lead electrically and the embedded one
mechanically. Exploration moves the regulator-count optimum to 76 cells with a different
topology and identifies the package decisions that reverse each ranking.
\end{abstract}
