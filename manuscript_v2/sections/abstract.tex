% ============================================================================ ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Kilowatt processors draw about a kiloampere at 1~V, so distributed vertical power delivery
places many regulator cells beneath the load. Where should these cells be integrated once
loss, temperature and deformation are solved together? Three integrations are
compared: embedded between substrate and die (Z1), on the substrate backside (Z2) and on the
board backside (Z3). An electrothermal--mechanical framework synthesizes the regulator from a
discrete part library and returns the cell count. On one package geometry it couples a
rail-aware loss ledger to temperature, solves the governing solder joint and checks every part
rating. For a 1-kW, 48-to-1-V load it selects \FrDseOptM{} hybrid
switched-capacitor cells switching at \FrConvFsw~kHz. Under one top cold plate, Z2 dissipates \GapAZtwo~W less than Z1
inside the package but \WrPlatGapA~W more once its 48-V feed is charged, and Z3 exceeds its
device ratings. With a bottom plate added, Z2 dissipates \GapBZtwo~W less than Z1 and peaks
\dTmaxTierBZtwo~K cooler, while Z3 exceeds the static voltage-drop limit. Z1 warps least under
both coolings, yet its hot corner joints accumulate the most inelastic strain; the joint of
largest displacement need not govern. The fine-pitch attaches of Z1 and Z2 warp far beyond
their bonding allowance. The die stays below \MthLimDie\,$^\circ$C under a
uniform load but reaches \FrDieHotMax\,$^\circ$C under a core-heavy power map. Further
requirements are interleaved input decoupling, copper posts through the 1-mm capacitor tier and,
at up to \FrTfiveSaveHi~W of extra loss, a converter short enough for the package-backside
board.
\end{abstract}
