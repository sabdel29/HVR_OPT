% ============================================================================ ABSTRACT
\begin{abstract}
Kilowatt processors draw about a kiloampere at 1~V, so distributed vertical power delivery
places many regulator cells beneath the load. Where should these cells be integrated once
loss, temperature and deformation are solved together? This paper compares three integrations:
embedded between substrate and die (Z1), on the package backside (Z2) and on the board backside
(Z3). An electrothermal--mechanical framework synthesizes the regulator from a discrete part
library and returns the cell count. On one package geometry, it couples a rail-aware loss ledger
to temperature, locates the governing solder joint and checks every part rating. For a 1-kW,
48-to-1-V load, it selects \FrDseOptM{} hybrid switched-capacitor cells switching at
\FrConvFsw~kHz. Under one top cold plate, Z2 dissipates \GapAZtwo~W less than Z1 inside the
package but \WrPlatGapA~W more once its 48-V feed is charged, and Z3 exceeds its device ratings.
With a bottom plate added, Z2 dissipates \GapBZtwo~W less than Z1 and peaks
\dTmaxTierBZtwo~K cooler, while Z3 exceeds the static voltage-drop limit. Z1 warps least under
both coolings, yet its hot corner joints accumulate the most inelastic strain, and the joint of
largest displacement need not govern. At their bonding temperature, the fine-pitch attaches of
Z1 and Z2 warp far beyond their allowance. The die stays below \MthLimDie\,$^\circ$C under a
uniform load but reaches \FrDieHotMax\,$^\circ$C under a core-heavy power map. The design also
needs interleaved input decoupling, copper posts through the 1-mm capacitor tier and, at up to
\FrTfiveSaveHi~W of extra loss, a converter short enough for the package-backside board.
\end{abstract}
