## 2. What the paper says, in six sentences

A framework evaluates a 1 kW, 48 V→1 V distributed vertical power delivery (DVPD) system —
converter synthesis, rail-aware power delivery, vertical interconnect geometry, package
thermal transport, thermoelastic deformation and a local solder submodel — all on **one**
physical stack rather than in separate downstream steps. Two integration alternatives are
compared: **Z1 embedded** (converter cells inside a 1.30 mm substrate, the vias carry 48 V)
and **Z2 backside** (cells under a 0.30 mm substrate, the same vias carry 1 V). Two tier
orderings that are equivalent in component count, area and stack height differ by a factor of
six in worst via aspect ratio (5.05 vs 0.833), so physical realizability is shown to be a
synthesis constraint, not a downstream check. On the resulting pair, Z2 runs **8.14 K hotter**
yet carries **18.4 % lower** local inelastic response — so temperature ranking and mechanical
ranking are **not** equivalent. The ordering is robust; the magnitude is conditional on a
solder model used far outside its calibration. **No damage, life or failure is claimed
anywhere** — this is the single most important thing to preserve in revision.

## 4. The four things a human must decide before submission

These are deliberately left open because they are not a tool's call:

1. **Author list, affiliations, acknowledgment, funding.** A provisional author line is in the
   `.tex` with a `% author list to be confirmed` comment.
2. **Venue formatting and page limit.** A generic IEEE journal class is used. The publisher's
   author instructions could **not** be reached from this machine, so the page limit was never
   verified. The paper is 15 pages. Also note `\balance` is currently commented out (with an
   explanatory comment) because it caused a 2.46 pt overfull box on the last page.
3. **Ratify or revert the title change.** The title used to read "Architecture-Dependent
   **Reliability Response**". An audit found that phrase appeared in the title and *nowhere
   else* in the paper, while the paper explicitly declines reliability/life claims. It now
   reads "Architecture-Dependent **Inelastic Response**". Reasoning: `TCPMT_HOSTILE_REVIEWER_AUDIT.md`
   section R11. Reverting is a one-line change to `\title{}` plus the keyword list.
4. **Page ranges for two references** (`Syed2004`, `Schubert2003`) — cited from this project's
   verified use of their calibrations, not checked against the originals.

## 5. What must NOT change (and why)

The numerical results are frozen. If a revision appears to require changing one, that is a
signal to stop and talk to Mohamed, not to recompute.

**Frozen final pair:**

| | Z1 embedded | Z2 backside |
|---|---:|---:|
| P_elec | 137.292 W | 139.659 W |
| T_max | 118.32 °C | 126.46 °C |
| corner-joint T | 112.75 °C | 120.30 °C |
| die warpage | 41.616 µm | 38.916 µm |
| Δu_c (displacement driver) | 4.6252 µm | 3.8714 µm |
| Δε_in (inelastic response) | 1.4072×10⁻² /cycle | 1.1478×10⁻² /cycle |

    D_u = 1.1947  (driver ratio,   16.3 % lower for Z2)
    D_ε = 1.2260  (response ratio, 18.4 % lower for Z2)

**Three terminology rules that must survive any rewrite:**

1. **Δu_c and Δε_in are different quantities and are never interchangeable.** Δu_c is a
   *displacement in micrometres* that drives the submodel; Δε_in is the *dimensionless strain
   increment per cycle* it returns. The 16.3 % belongs only to Δu_c; the 18.4 % only to Δε_in.
   Conflating them was a real defect that was found and fixed — please do not reintroduce it.
2. **Response ≠ damage ≠ life ≠ failure.** The paper computes response and stops. Words like
   "damage", "more reliable", "lasts longer", "cycles to failure" are forbidden. The six
   existing uses of "damage" are all disclaimers.
3. **Ordering is the result; magnitude is an envelope.** The solder model is evaluated at
   15–17× yield against a fit over 7.51–15.92 MPa. Do not soften or delete that limitation.

An automated check enforces all of this — see section 6.

## 8. Practical warnings, learned the hard way

* **Do not run two pytest sessions at once.** One test (`test_reports_regenerate_idempotently`)
  rebuilds shared report files and PDFs. Running it concurrently with anything else produces a
  failure that looks like a regression but is not. Run test suites one at a time.
* **`python3` is not the project Python.** Without the PATH export you will get
  `ModuleNotFoundError`, or `ccx: Permission denied` from a different `ccx` earlier in PATH.
* **~2.7 GB of solver scratch** sits in `results/phase0mr3c/local_final/` (1.8 G) and
  `results/phase0mr3c/cetol_sweep/` (898 M). These are raw CalculiX outputs. The summarised
  CSVs next to them contain everything the paper uses, so the scratch can be deleted to
  reclaim space and regenerated later with the scripts in section 7:

      rm -rf results/phase0mr3c/local_final results/phase0mr3c/cetol_sweep

* **No internet from this host** for publisher sites. IEEE Xplore returns HTTP 418 to scripted
  access. arXiv and Crossref APIs do work, which is how the references were verified.
